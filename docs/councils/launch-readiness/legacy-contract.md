## Proposed lifecycle contract, subject to Q07

1. **Current member:** may see only their current household's approved shared content; private planning stays own-account only. Separate member-view identity from invite administration. If sharing is retained, designate an authorized inviter/remover and provide explicit code rotation/revoke; do not infer authority solely from the existing `role` text until backed by server checks. A member removal immediately stops direct API access and invalidates invitations issued under the old grant. Existing data remains stable during a rollout.
2. **Former member:** no household reads/writes solely on past membership. Any export extension is **Q07 policy pending** and limited to demonstrably attributable facts: for example, a retained dated entry with `added_by` equal to the requester, or a separately authored unmerged item if its provenance can be verified. For a merged grocery row, `added_by` alone does not prove the requester's share of the quantity or sources; do not export its aggregate details or other members' contributions as the former member's data. If provenance cannot be demonstrated, disclose the limitation without exposing co-member merged grocery details. Decide whether their past shared contributions remain visible to current members, are redacted, or are removed where separable. A historical membership ledger or equivalent would be needed to explain former-household scope; merely querying `HouseholdMember` cannot do so after `leave`.
3. **Last member:** `leave` and account deletion need a consistent outcome: explicit archive/retention or deletion of now-unowned household content, with a user-visible explanation. Do not silently leave an indefinitely joinable orphan behind or delete co-member data. Handle concurrent last-member operations in one transaction with locking and rollback proof. Preserve creator records until the approved choice is executable.
4. **Deleted owner:** revoke owned public links and all sessions, remove own private data, and retain only Q07-approved shared content with attribution removed. Co-member data must survive. If the deleted person was the only household owner, require an approved transfer/closure rule; a remaining member's content must not become public by default. A deleted user's display name must not remain in a public response.
5. **Public identity and invitations:** proposed default is an explicit, revocable named-plan link that exposes a plan title and allowed item summaries but no owner email or display name unless Q07 approves attribution. Return the same 404 for missing, expired and revoked slugs; no redirect or cached public body after revocation. Rotate/expire household invite secrets, rate-limit code attempts, restrict code disclosure to authorized inviters, and record invitation/revocation semantics. The exact public fields and who may invite/remove are product decisions, not implicit engineering defaults.
6. **Export/delete/retention:** inventory private planning, preferences, previews, undo/inverse and mutation receipts along with older user, favorite, cook, reflection, named-plan, member, grocery and dated-entry relationships ([account-data-inventory.md](account-data-inventory.md), [planning-production-track.md:167](planning-production-track.md#L167)). Export only `own` or `shared-attributed` facts when attribution is demonstrable and Q07 allows disclosure. For merged or unknown-provenance rows, explain the limitation in export metadata; do **not** include co-member aggregate quantities, source lists or details in a former-member export merely because `added_by` matches. Define the deletion and backup/receipt retention period, restoration-after-erasure procedure, notices and operator responsibilities with Q07/operator review. No numeric retention period is approved here. Never copy a shared record into a single member's private workspace to simplify deletion.

## Content sync and partial visibility contract for D12/D04

Current CLI order is separate `sync-recipes`, `sync-glossary`, `sync-skills`, `sync-lessons` commands ([app.py:135](../../../app.py#L135)); recipe, glossary, skills and lessons each commit independently ([sync_recipes.py:220](../../../scripts/sync_recipes.py#L220), [sync_glossary.py:81](../../../scripts/sync_glossary.py#L81), [sync_learning.py:174](../../../scripts/sync_learning.py#L174), [sync_learning.py:230](../../../scripts/sync_learning.py#L230)). A later lesson failure can therefore leave earlier recipe/glossary/skill changes visible. Lessons validate links before their own writes, but that does not make the four-command release atomic ([sync_learning.py:190](../../../scripts/sync_learning.py#L190)). Private catalog records have authored revisions and per-record availability gates ([planning_catalog.py:247](../../../planning_catalog.py#L247), [planning_catalog.py:326](../../../planning_catalog.py#L326)); they are not a single global release switch for all legacy content.

**Required observable contract, proposed:** a failed late content step leaves normal reads on the prior complete EN/DE recipe, glossary, skill, lesson and authored-catalog set; a successful candidate appears as one coherent set, never a mix of partly committed sync phases. Existing cook snapshots and account/private records stay readable under their own access rules. A saved configured item whose exact authored revision is unavailable returns an explicit unavailable state; never substitute another recipe, language or tier, and never silently discard selected options ([planning_catalog.py:326](../../../planning_catalog.py#L326), [planning.py:569](../../../routes/planning.py#L569)). Retried publication must preserve revocations and creator records. A recovery build must read **and write** records created by the candidate build. D04's proposed [release-runbook.md](release-runbook.md) supplies ordering, old-client assets and incident procedure; [recovery-test-design.md](recovery-test-design.md) supplies the isolated candidate/recovery-build proof. Neither specifies an approved backend architecture.

| Architecture for D12 to decide and test | How it satisfies the visibility contract | Cost/condition |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Coordinated single transaction** | Parse/validate all four content domains and catalog dependencies, then run one serialized database transaction across every update/insert and commit once. Replace per-sync commits with flushes under one orchestrator and ensure no independent sync job can publish mid-run. A failure before commit rolls back all content writes. | Likely smaller than versioning every content table. The current four CLI commands cannot share this transaction; long locks, migration ordering, retries, read consistency and changed in-place recipe rows need measured tests. If a request needs a coherent multi-query view across commit, use a consistent read snapshot or equivalent. Active cook snapshots remain pinned separately. |
| **B. Staged revisions plus active pointer** | Write candidate revisions behind a visibility gate and atomically advance the active release pointer only after all validation. Earlier staging commits may exist but cannot serve normal reads. | Supports long preparation and explicit release identity, but requires version-aware reads/relationships across all content domains, retention/cleanup and old-client compatibility. Existing per-record catalog availability is insufficient as a global gate. |

**Architecture decision is open**, not approved by this ADR. D12 should choose after a bounded design and PostgreSQL fault-injection comparison. A must prove one DB commit covers every content write and that a failure at the final lesson phase rolls back earlier recipe/glossary/skill changes; B must prove earlier committed staged rows are invisible until activation and failed activation leaves the old pointer. Both must pin artifact/content identity, serialize runs, reject changed-digest reruns without writes, preserve existing revocations, and keep candidate-created account/planning rows writable by the designated recovery build. Schema migration is a separate compatibility gate; content atomicity alone does not roll a migration back.

Synthetic late-failure test: seed prior complete content plus A/B saved records; start candidate sync with changed EN/DE recipes and glossary, inject failure at the last lesson phase. Current expected state is potentially mixed because earlier commands committed. Target under A: no content commit, unchanged prior rows and normal reads. Target under B: staged candidate rows may exist but all normal reads still use the prior active set. In either case, no candidate-only catalog entry is eligible, private records and old cook snapshot are unchanged, and a corrected identical candidate can publish once without reopening revoked content. Then prove a designated recovery build can read **and write** a candidate-created plan/receipt after isolated restore, and replay post-archive account/link revocations; D04's [test stages C, E and F](recovery-test-design.md#scenario-and-assertions) require this. The current D04 stage-C wording assumes earlier sync **commits**, so it should be parameterized: inject before A's single commit or after B's earlier staging commits, with an independent reader asserting the same visibility invariant. No such rehearsal was run for D01.
