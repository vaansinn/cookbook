# Pilot Observation Materials — #39a

For the first teaching-pilot slice: one dish (Lentil Bolognese, Basic), one skill (Simmering, attached
to step `rinse-and-simmer`). Two parts below: a **content review** for an experienced cook to sanity-check
the lesson before any beginner sees it, and an **observation sheet** for watching 1-2 beginners actually
cook with it.

Gate per `PIPELINE.md` #39a: this whole document is gated on the content review passing before any
beginner session is scheduled.

---

## Part 1 — Content review checklist (experienced cook, before any beginner sees this)

Reviewer: an experienced home cook or someone from the team who actually cooks, not just reads recipes.
Reads `content/lessons/simmering/en.md` (and `de.md` if a German-speaking reviewer is available) plus the
recipe it attaches to, `content/recipes/lentil-bolognese/basic.en.md`, side by side.

- [ ] **Does the simmer description match how you'd actually describe it?** Read the "success cue"
  paragraph out loud. Is "a few small bubbles breaking the surface, a handful every second" how you'd
  coach someone standing at your stove, or does it undersell/oversell what a correct simmer looks like
  for this specific pot of lentils and water?
- [ ] **Is the recovery guidance actually correct for this dish?** If you deliberately let a test batch of
  this recipe's lentils boil hard for 2-3 minutes, do they in fact go mushy by the 12-15 min mark the way
  the lesson claims? If you deliberately keep the heat too low, does "no bubbles for a minute or two, turn
  it up slightly" actually get you back to a normal simmer without overshooting into a hard boil?
- [ ] **Timing check**: does 12-15 minutes on a normal home stove, at the simmer level described, actually
  produce properly tender (not crunchy, not mush) lentils with this dish's water ratio (double volume)?
- [ ] **The equipment/parallel-task concern** (flagged during the content audit — see handback): step 1 says
  "simmer... 12-15 min... Drain," and step 2 says "**Meanwhile**, boil the spaghetti," but the recipe's own
  Notes say the pot is used "one after the other" (lentils, then pasta), and `equipment` in the front matter
  lists only `[one pot]`. As written, "meanwhile" is not literally possible with one pot occupied by
  simmering lentils for 12-15 minutes. **Confirm with an actual stovetop run**: is this genuinely
  sequential (start the pasta water only after the lentils are draining), and if so, should the recipe's
  step 2 wording change from "Meanwhile" to something sequential (e.g. "Once the lentils are draining...")
  before beginners see it? Flag this back to whoever owns recipe content — it's a recipe-text fix, not a
  lesson-content fix, and it sits outside this content's scope to correct unilaterally.
- [ ] **Tone check**: does the lesson read as warm/encouraging (per `business.md`), or does any line read as
  condescending to someone who has genuinely never done this before?
- [ ] **Next-practice check**: is Eggs Benedict (Basic) — extending the same simmer skill into a *bare*
  simmer for poaching — actually a sensible, achievable next step for someone who just finished this
  lesson, or does it introduce too much at once (a new poaching technique, egg handling)?
- [ ] **German version**: if reviewed, does `de.md` read as something a real person would say, not a literal
  translation of the English (register, word choice — "köcheln" vs. a stiffer alternative)?

**Outcome of this review**: pass / needs changes (list them) / needs a stovetop re-test before it can be
judged. Record the reviewer's name and date here before scheduling Part 2.

## Part 2 — Beginner observation sheet

One sheet per beginner. Reviewer sits with the cook (in person or on video) and takes notes live —
don't rely on memory afterward. Keep to what you can actually see or hear; skip anything that requires
guessing at what they're thinking.

**Session info**
- Participant: __________ Date: __________ Recorded (Y/N): __________
- Has this person cooked from a written recipe unsupervised before? Y / N
- Has this person made a simmering-type dish (rice, soup, pasta sauce) before, to their own knowledge? Y / N

### A. Hesitation points
Note the step number and what happened, not just "seemed unsure."

| Step | Paused / re-read the text? | Visibly hesitated before acting? | What they did next |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rinsing lentils | | | |
| Starting the simmer (step 1) | | | |
| Judging the simmer level once it's going | | | |
| Deciding when to start the pasta (step 2) | | | |
| Judging pasta doneness | | | |
| Combining sauce + lentils (step 3) | | | |

- [ ] Did they read the lesson content *before* starting to cook, *during* (mid-step), or not at all
  (only the recipe)?
- [ ] Did they re-open/re-read the lesson content more than once during the simmer step? How many times?

### B. Outside help
Concrete, observable actions only — not "did they feel confident."

- [ ] Asked another person in the room/household for help or reassurance. If yes, what did they ask?
- [ ] Looked something up outside the app (search engine, video, another recipe site). If yes, what were
  they searching for?
- [ ] Re-read the same lesson passage more than twice.
- [ ] Asked the reviewer directly for help or confirmation (note: reviewer should not volunteer help
  unless the food is at real risk of being ruined — the point is to see what they do unaided).
- [ ] Used a tool not mentioned in the recipe (thermometer, timer app, etc.) to compensate for uncertainty.

### C. Self-assessed success
Can they tell, unprompted, that the simmer is right — this is the core thing this lesson is meant to teach.

- [ ] At some point during step 1, did they say or visibly indicate "this looks right" / "this is a
  simmer" **without being asked**? Quote what they said if possible.
- [ ] If asked directly ("how do you know this is simmering and not boiling?"), can they point to a
  specific visual cue from the lesson (bubble size/frequency), or do they answer vaguely ("it just looks
  right")?
- [ ] Did they check the pot at all during the 12-15 minutes, or set it and walk away without checking?
  How many times did they check?
- [ ] At the end of step 1, did they believe the lentils turned out correctly (their own judgment, asked
  before you tell them anything)? Right / too soft / too firm / not sure.

### D. Recovery (only applicable if the simmer actually went wrong during this session)
- [ ] Did they notice the heat was too high/low **on their own**, or only when prompted/asked to check?
- [ ] Did they take an action to correct it? What did they do?
- [ ] Did the lesson's recovery guidance match what they actually needed in the moment, or was it missing
  something (e.g. didn't cover their specific situation)?

### E. Next-practice reception
Do this *after* the cook is finished and served, as a separate short conversation — not mid-cook.

- [ ] Show them the next_practice suggestion (Eggs Benedict, Basic, "same skill, bare simmer for
  poaching"). Read the reason aloud.
- [ ] Do they say they understand *why* this is being suggested (i.e. the connection to what they just
  practiced), in their own words?
- [ ] Does it sound appealing to them as a next dish, independent of the teaching angle? Yes / no / unsure
  — note their actual reaction.
- [ ] Would they actually attempt it this week, in their own estimate? Yes / maybe / no.
- [ ] Any dish they'd have expected or wanted to be suggested instead?

## Debrief questions (ask the beginner directly, after cooking, in this order)

1. "Before you started, what did you think 'simmer' meant?" (baseline, don't correct them yet)
2. "Walk me through how you knew it was simmering, not boiling, once it was going." (should surface the
   same cue from section C — see if it matches unprompted)
3. "Was there any point you weren't sure what to do next?" (cross-check against section A/B — did they
   report something the reviewer didn't catch, or vice versa)
4. "If you cooked this again with no lesson text at all, what would you do differently?"
5. "Does Eggs Benedict sound like something you'd actually want to cook next?" (cross-check against
   section E)

## After the session — reviewer summary (one paragraph per beginner)
Free text: overall impression of whether the lesson content did its job (taught the visual cue for a
simmer, was consultable without breaking their flow, gave usable recovery guidance if needed), plus
anything that should change before the next slice of beginners or before #34a-#38a builds the sync/UI
around this content.
