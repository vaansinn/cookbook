## Prep ahead
- This one spans two days: autolyse and a short rise on day one, a cold overnight ferment, then bake on day two
- Have a spray bottle of water or a few ice cubes ready for the steam step

## Ingredients
- 400|3 cups flour|flour_wheat
- 3.5|1/2 tsp instant yeast|yeast_dry
- 12|2 tsp salt|salt

## Instructions
1. Autolyse: stir the flour with ~330 ml water only, no yeast or salt yet, until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 30 min — the flour hydrates and starts building gluten on its own.
2. Sprinkle the yeast and salt over the dough and fold them in with wet hands. Then stretch-and-fold: grab one edge, stretch it up and fold it to the centre, turn the bowl a quarter turn, repeat 4 times. This builds gluten strength without traditional kneading.
3. Cover and refrigerate 12-16 hours — the cold, slow ferment is what gives this loaf real depth of flavour, not just rise.
4. The next day, turn the cold dough onto a floured surface, shape into a taut ball (fold edges to centre, then drag to build tension), and let it rest, covered, 1 hour at room temperature while the oven preheats.
5. Preheat the oven to 230°C (450°F) with the empty covered pot inside for 30 min. Score the dough with one clean slash. Drop it into the hot pot, mist the inside of the pot with water or drop in 2-3 ice cubes before covering — the steam keeps the crust flexible long enough for a full oven-spring.
6. Bake covered 25 min, then uncovered 20-25 min more until deeply golden. Cool on a rack at least 30 min before slicing — it's still cooking internally as it cools.

## Notes
- The cold overnight ferment (retarding) is the single biggest lever for flavour in home bread baking — the same dough baked same-day tastes noticeably flatter.
- Steam in the first few minutes keeps the crust soft enough for the dough to keep expanding (oven spring) before it sets — skip it and the loaf stays smaller and denser.
- Equipment: one bowl (mixing, first rise) + one heavy lidded pot (baking) + a sharp blade (scoring).
