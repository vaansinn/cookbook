## Prep ahead
- Clarify the butter first — it holds heat better in the hollandaise and won't water the sauce down with milk solids
- Set up your mise en place completely before poaching a single egg: this dish lives or dies on three things landing hot at the same moment
- Have a warm (not hot) plate ready — everything goes cold fast once it's plated

## Ingredients
- 114|2 English muffins, split|english_muffin
- 100|4 slices Canadian bacon or ham|ham
- 200|4 eggs, for poaching|eggs
- 15|1 tbsp white vinegar|vinegar_white
- 34|2 egg yolks, for the hollandaise|egg_yolk
- 125|125 g butter, clarified|butter
- 8|1/2 lemon, juiced|lemon
- 0.9|A pinch of cayenne pepper|cayenne_pepper
- 3|A pinch of chives, finely chopped|chives_fresh

## Instructions
1. Clarify the butter: melt it gently and skim off the white foam on top, then pour off the clear golden fat, leaving the milky solids behind in the pan. Keep it warm but not hot.
2. Set a bowl over a bain-marie of barely simmering water — the bowl shouldn't touch the water. Whisk the egg yolks with a splash of water until pale and slightly thickened, 2-3 min.
3. Off the heat, drizzle in the clarified butter drop by drop at first, whisking constantly, then in a thin stream once it starts to thicken — this is the same emulsion as any hollandaise, just built entirely by hand so you can feel the exact moment it's ready. Whisk in the lemon juice and cayenne. Keep it warm over the (now off) bain-marie, whisking occasionally so it doesn't set.
4. Toast the muffins and warm the ham — time these to finish within a minute of the eggs.
5. Poach the eggs in barely-simmering vinegar water, all four close together if your pot is wide enough, 3 min for a runny yolk.
6. Assemble immediately: muffin, ham, egg, a generous spoon of hollandaise, chives. Serve the moment it's plated.

## Notes
- The whole dish is a timing exercise more than a difficulty one — clarified butter and hollandaise both hold their heat well, which buys you the minute you need to poach and assemble.
- A too-hot bain-marie is the single most common way to scramble hollandaise — if it's ever steaming hard, lift the bowl off for a few seconds.
- Equipment: one pot (poaching) + one pan (ham, and clarifying the butter) + a heatproof bowl (hollandaise).
