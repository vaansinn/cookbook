## Prep ahead
- Weigh the rice rather than measuring by volume — sushi rice is precise about ratios, and cups pack differently depending on how you scoop
- If you have a wooden hangiri or sushi tub, soak it in cold water for 10 min first so it doesn't pull moisture out of the rice
- Warm the seasoning gently rather than boiling it — boiling drives off the vinegar's aroma along with the water

## Ingredients
- 300|300 g short-grain sushi rice, weighed|sushi_rice_dry
- 45|3 tbsp rice vinegar|rice_vinegar
- 25|2 tbsp sugar|sugar
- 6|1 tsp salt|salt

## Instructions
1. Rinse the rice in a bowl, swirling with your hand, changing water each time until it runs nearly clear — usually 4-5 changes. Over-rinsing strips starch you actually want; stop once the water is mostly clear, not perfectly clear.
2. Drain in a sieve and let it sit exactly 20-30 min. This rest lets the grains hydrate evenly before cooking, which is most of what separates gluey rice from properly separate grains.
3. Cook with water at a 1:1.1 ratio by weight (330 g water for 300 g rice). Bring to a boil uncovered, then cover, drop to the lowest simmer, and cook exactly 12 min without lifting the lid — every peek loses steam the rice needs.
4. Rest, still covered, off the heat for 10 min. Do not skip this — it's when the residual steam finishes the centre of each grain.
5. Warm the vinegar, sugar and salt together just until the sugar dissolves, well below a simmer — a flame or a few seconds in the microwave is enough.
6. Tip the hot rice into the dampened wooden tub (or a wide non-metal bowl — metal can react with the vinegar). Pour the seasoning over the back of a rice paddle so it spreads rather than pools in one spot.
7. Fold with a slicing, cutting motion — never stir or mash, that crushes the grains. Fan continuously as you fold; this cools the rice fast enough to give it a glossy sheen instead of a dull, soggy one.
8. Taste-test by pressing a grain between two fingers: it should hold its shape but yield without resistance. Cover with a damp cloth until you're ready to use it — sushi rice dries out fast at room temperature.

## Notes
- This is the same ingredient list as the everyday version — sushi rice is a technique dish more than an ingredient one. The gap between good and great is almost entirely in the resting times and the folding motion.
- A metal bowl can give the vinegar a faint tinny taste over time — wood, glass, or plastic are the traditional choices for a reason.
- Yields ~700 g seasoned rice — enough for 6 rolls or a generous nigiri plate for two.
- Equipment: one pot + a wooden hangiri or wide non-metal bowl.
