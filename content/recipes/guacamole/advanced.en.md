## Prep ahead
- Toast the cumin seeds before anything else — they need a minute in a dry pan and a moment to cool before grinding
- Finely dice the onion, tomato, and jalapeño; check the avocados are ripe

## Ingredients
- 480|3 avocados, halved and pitted|avocado
- 30|Juice of 1-2 limes|lime
- 50|1/4 onion, finely diced|onion
- 100|1 tomato, seeded and diced|tomato_fresh
- 10|1 tbsp cilantro, chopped|coriander_fresh
- 5|1 garlic clove|garlic
- 15|1/2 jalapeño, finely diced|jalapeno_fresh
- 1.05|1/2 tsp cumin seeds, toasted and ground|cumin_ground
- 6|1 tsp salt|salt

## Instructions
1. Toast the cumin seeds in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often, 1-2 min until fragrant. Cool, then grind in a mortar and pestle (or crush with the flat of a knife).
2. In the mortar (or a heavy bowl), mash the onion, garlic, jalapeño, and a pinch of the salt together into a rough paste first — this is the traditional molcajete order, and it builds a flavour base the avocado can't create on its own.
3. Add the avocado in chunks and mash it into the paste, working in the lime juice as you go, until it reaches your preferred texture — hand-mashed guacamole should still have visible chunks, never fully smooth.
4. Fold in the tomato, cilantro, and toasted cumin. Taste and adjust salt and lime.

## Notes
- Building the aromatics into a paste before adding the avocado (rather than just mixing everything together at once) layers the flavour instead of leaving it flat.
- A mortar and pestle (molcajete in Mexican kitchens) gives a rustic texture a food processor can't — it crushes rather than purées.
- Equipment: a mortar and pestle (or a heavy bowl and the back of a spoon).
