## Prep ahead
- Let the coconut milk can sit undisturbed (or chill it) so the cream fully separates from the thinner liquid
- Press the tofu between paper towels for 10 min before cubing — it fries and holds its shape better with less water in it
- Have every component ready before you start; this dish moves fast once the pot is hot

## Ingredients
- 400|1 can (400 ml) coconut milk, cream separated|coconut_milk
- 300|300 g firm tofu, pressed and cubed|tofu_firm
- 13.5|1 tbsp oil, for frying the tofu|olive_oil
- 60|4 tbsp green curry paste|curry_paste_green_jarred
- 120|1 bell pepper, sliced|bell_pepper
- 18|1 tbsp fish sauce|fish_sauce
- 6|1 tsp sugar|sugar
- 250|250 g rice|rice_dry
- 8|A handful of Thai basil leaves|thai_basil
- 8|1/2 lime, cut into wedges|lime

## Instructions
1. Cook the rice: rinse, then simmer covered in ~1.5x its volume of water for 10-12 min, rest 5 min off the heat.
2. Pan-fry the pressed tofu cubes in the oil over medium-high heat until golden on a couple of sides, 4-5 min. Set aside — this gives it a texture that just simmering in curry never will.
3. Scoop the thick coconut cream into the same pot. Heat until it starts to split slightly, then fry the curry paste in it, stirring, 2-3 min until deeply fragrant and the oil starts separating out at the edges — a sign the paste is properly cooked, not just heated.
4. Add the bell pepper and the remaining coconut milk. Simmer 8 min, then return the fried tofu just to warm through — adding it late keeps its fried texture instead of turning it soft again.
5. Season with fish sauce and sugar, balancing salty-sweet to taste. Off the heat, fold in the basil. Serve over rice with lime.

## Notes
- Frying the tofu separately before it goes anywhere near the curry is the single biggest texture upgrade over just simmering it — worth the extra pan.
- The paste is properly bloomed when you see a thin layer of oil separate at the edges of the pot — that's the fat carrying the aromatics, not just heat passing through.
- Equipment: one pot (tofu can be fried in the same pot first, then set aside).
