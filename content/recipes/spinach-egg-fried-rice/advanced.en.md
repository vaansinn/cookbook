## Prep ahead
- Have every ingredient chopped and within arm's reach before the wok goes on — once it's hot, this cooks in minutes and there's no time to chop mid-stir-fry
- Break up the cold rice with wet hands so the grains separate cleanly instead of staying in clumps
- Beat one egg fully, but keep a second egg's yolk aside to coat the rice separately

## Ingredients
- 400|~400 g cooked cold rice, day-old|rice_cooked
- 100|2 eggs, beaten|eggs
- 50|1 egg yolk, beaten separately|eggs
- 100|100 g spinach, roughly chopped|spinach_fresh
- 120|1 red bell pepper, diced|bell_pepper
- 30|2 spring onions, whites and greens separated, sliced|spring_onion
- 5|1 garlic clove, minced|garlic
- 32|2 tbsp soy sauce|soy_sauce
- 20|1.5 tbsp oil, divided|olive_oil
- 4.5|1 tsp sesame oil|sesame_oil
- 1.8|A pinch of chili flakes|chili_flakes

## Instructions
1. Get your wok smoking hot over the highest heat your stove has — this is what stir-frying actually needs, and it's the step most home versions skip.
2. Toss the cold rice with the egg yolk in a bowl until every grain is lightly coated. This is the "golden fried rice" technique — it keeps the rice separate and gives it a light golden colour without extra oil.
3. Add 1 tbsp oil to the wok, pour in the beaten eggs, and scramble in large soft curds. Remove immediately — they'll keep cooking off the heat.
4. Add the remaining oil, then the spring onion whites and garlic — 15 seconds, no more, garlic burns fast at this heat.
5. Add the bell pepper, stir-fry 1 min, then add the yolk-coated rice. Spread it against the hot sides of the wok and let it sit a few seconds at a time rather than stirring constantly — this is what builds "wok hei", the light char flavour proper fried rice has.
6. Push everything to one side, add the spinach to the cleared space, let it wilt 30 sec, then toss together with the egg and soy sauce along the edge of the wok, not poured over the top.
7. Finish with the spring onion greens, sesame oil, and chili flakes, tossed through off the heat.

## Notes
- A properly hot wok is the single biggest difference between restaurant and home fried rice — if it's not smoking before the oil goes in, it's not hot enough.
- Coating the rice in egg yolk before frying (not after) is what makes each grain glisten gold instead of the egg just sitting in scrambled clumps.
- Adding sauce along the hot metal edge of the wok, not directly onto the food, flash-caramelises it slightly before it mixes in.
- Equipment: one wok, ideally carbon steel for the heat retention this technique needs.
