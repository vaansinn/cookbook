## Prep ahead
- Bloom the saffron in a splash of warm water as your first step — it needs 10 min to release its colour and aroma before it goes anywhere near the pan
- Dice the potatoes small and even so they sear rather than steam
- Have the sherry vinegar measured — it goes in at the very end, off heat

## Ingredients
- 500|500 g potatoes, diced small|potatoes
- 240|1 can chickpeas (400 g), drained|chickpeas_canned
- 110|1 onion, finely chopped|onion
- 10|2 garlic cloves, chopped|garlic
- 400|1 can chopped tomatoes (400 g)|tomatoes_canned
- 2.3|1 tsp smoked paprika|smoked_paprika
- 2.1|1 tsp ground cumin|cumin_ground
- 0.1|A generous pinch of saffron threads, bloomed in 2 tbsp warm water|saffron
- 15|1 tbsp sherry vinegar|sherry_vinegar
- 27|2 tbsp olive oil|olive_oil
- 100|100 g feta, crumbled|feta
- 100|2 eggs, poached|eggs
- 10|Fresh parsley|coriander_fresh

## Instructions
1. Steep the saffron threads in the warm water and set aside — do this first, it needs the full 10 min.
2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the potatoes in a single layer and let them sit undisturbed 3-4 min before turning, so they properly sear rather than just steam. Continue until browned and nearly tender, 12-15 min total.
3. Push the potatoes aside, add the onion to the cleared space, and cook 5 min. Add the garlic, paprika and cumin, 1 min until fragrant.
4. Add the chickpeas, chopped tomatoes, and the bloomed saffron with its soaking water. Simmer 10-12 min until the potatoes are fully tender and the sauce has reduced and thickened.
5. Meanwhile, poach the eggs: simmer water with a splash of vinegar, swirl, and slide in the eggs for 2-3 min until the whites set but yolks stay runny.
6. Off the heat, stir in the sherry vinegar — this is the finishing acid that lifts the whole dish, added last so it stays bright instead of cooking off.
7. Crumble the feta on top, place a poached egg on each portion, scatter parsley, and serve straight from the pan.

## Notes
- Saffron needs to steep in warm liquid before cooking — dropped in dry, most of its colour and aroma never releases.
- Adding acid (the sherry vinegar) at the very end rather than during cooking keeps its brightness instead of mellowing it into the background.
- Searing the potatoes undisturbed, rather than stirring constantly, is what gets a real crust instead of soft-fried cubes.
- Equipment: one large skillet + a small pot for poaching.
