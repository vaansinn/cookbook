## Prep ahead
- Slice more onions than feels reasonable — they cook down to a fraction of their raw volume
- Budget the full hour for caramelizing; there's no real shortcut to it
- Have both the wine and stock measured out, since deglazing happens more than once

## Ingredients
- 660|6 onions, thinly sliced|onion
- 42|3 tbsp butter|butter
- 90|90 ml white wine, divided|white_wine
- 600|600 ml beef stock|beef_stock
- 1.5|1/2 tsp dried thyme|herbs_dried
- 6|1 tsp salt|salt
- 1.15|1/2 tsp black pepper|black_pepper
- 60|2 slices baguette|baguette
- 100|100 g Gruyère, grated|cheese_gruyere

## Instructions
1. Melt the butter in a heavy pot over medium-low heat. Add the onions with a pinch of the salt (it helps draw out moisture early) and cook, stirring every few minutes, 50-60 min total.
2. As a dark fond starts building on the pot's bottom, deglaze in stages: add a splash of the wine, scrape it up, let it cook off, and keep going. This happens 2-3 times over the hour, and it's what builds real depth rather than just soft, sweet onions.
3. Once the onions are a deep mahogany colour and completely jammy, add the stock, remaining wine, and thyme. Simmer 20 min. Season with salt and pepper.
4. Ladle into oven-safe bowls, top with baguette and a generous layer of Gruyère. Broil, watching closely, until deeply bubbled and browned in spots — a minute of inattention burns it.

## Notes
- The colour of the onions at the end (deep mahogany, not just golden) is the real marker of a fully caramelized soup — golden means good, mahogany means great.
- Deglazing in stages rather than once at the end captures fond that would otherwise just build up and eventually scorch.
- Equipment: one heavy pot (even heat distribution matters here) + oven-safe bowls.
