## Prep ahead
- Toss the chickpeas with yogurt and spices and let them sit while you prep everything else — even 10 min helps them take on flavour
- Finely chop the onion, mince the garlic and ginger into a paste
- Measure the ground spices into one small bowl before the pan is hot — once it's blooming, it moves fast

## Ingredients
- 250|250 g rice (basmati or long grain)|rice_dry
- 480|2 cans chickpeas, drained|chickpeas_canned
- 60|60 g plain yogurt (for the chickpeas)|yogurt_plain
- 60|60 g plain yogurt (to finish the sauce)|yogurt_plain
- 110|1 onion, finely chopped|onion
- 10|2 garlic cloves, minced to a paste|garlic
- 15|A thumb of ginger, minced to a paste|ginger_fresh
- 32|2 tbsp tomato paste|tomato_paste
- 4|2 tsp garam masala|garam_masala
- 2.2|1 tsp ground turmeric|turmeric_ground
- 1.8|1 tsp chili flakes, or to taste|chili_flakes
- 27|2 tbsp oil, divided|olive_oil
- 50|A handful of spinach|spinach_fresh
- 10|Fresh coriander, to serve|coriander_fresh

## Instructions
1. Toss the drained chickpeas with the first portion of yogurt, half the garam masala, and a pinch of the turmeric. Set aside to marinate while you prep.
2. Cook the rice: rinse, then simmer covered in ~1.5x its volume of water for 10-12 min, rest 5 min off the heat.
3. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a wide pan over fairly high heat. Tip in the marinated chickpeas in one layer and let them sit undisturbed 2-3 min until they catch a little colour and char at the edges — this is where the flavour comes from, don't stir too soon. Remove and set aside.
4. Add the remaining oil to the same pan, soften the onion 6-7 min. Add garlic and ginger paste, 1 min, until fragrant.
5. Stir in the tomato paste, remaining garam masala, turmeric, and chili flakes. Fry 1-2 min — this blooms the spices in fat, which is what makes the sauce taste layered instead of raw.
6. Add a splash of water to loosen, then return the chickpeas and simmer 6-8 min until the sauce thickens and coats them.
7. Off the heat, swirl in the remaining yogurt (this is what makes it creamy without cream) and the spinach until wilted. Serve over rice with coriander.

## Notes
- Marinating and pan-charring the chickpeas before saucing them is what separates this from just simmering them in sauce — it builds a base layer of flavour the jarred version skips entirely.
- Blooming ground spices in hot oil (not water) unlocks their aroma — always fry them briefly before adding liquid.
- Swap the yogurt finish for a splash of cream for a richer, more classic "makhani" style.
- Equipment: one pot (rice) + one wide pan.
